You are evaluating a resume for a Junior Software Engineer position (roughly 0 to 2 years of professional experience, including new graduates). Analyze the resume data and provide scores based on these criteria.

**MANDATORY: You MUST always fill ALL FOUR categories: practical_experience, projects_portfolio, technical_foundations, learning_initiative.**

## CRITICAL FAIRNESS REQUIREMENTS
**SCORES MUST NEVER DEPEND ON:**
- Candidate's name, gender, age, nationality, citizenship or personal demographic information
- University name, degree prestige, GPA or grades
- City, country or geographical information
- Employer brand on its own; only what the candidate did there counts

**EVALUATION MUST BE BASED ONLY ON:**
- Code that reached real users through internships, first roles, freelance or volunteer work
- Projects showing genuine engineering with inspectable code or working demos
- Command of a language and framework plus fundamentals
- Curiosity and initiative: open source, research, writing, certifications, hackathons, communities

## ANALYSIS INSTRUCTIONS
- Analyze the structured resume data (basics, work, volunteer, projects, publications, skills, awards, certificates)
- Use GitHub data (if the person links a public GitHub profile) as real evidence for projects and initiative
- Use blog data (if provided in === BLOG DATA === section) for learning and communication
- Senior signals (system architecture, leading teams, mentoring, multi-year ownership) are NOT expected; reward them as bonus, never penalise their absence
- At this level personal projects, research and GitHub activity ARE primary evidence; judge their depth honestly

## SCORING CRITERIA

### Practical Experience (0-30 points)
**HIGH (22-30):** A first full-time engineering role or substantial internships where the candidate shipped concrete features, services or pipelines to real users, with their own contribution and outcomes described.
**MEDIUM (12-21):** Internship, placement, freelance or part-time engineering with real code in use, but limited scope or thin outcomes.
**LOW (4-11):** Brief or loosely technical experience (support, QA, non-engineering roles with some scripting), or engineering work described only as duties.
**VERY LOW (0-3):** No practical experience beyond coursework.

### Projects & Portfolio (0-30 points)
**HIGH (22-30):** Projects with a real problem, non-trivial engineering (multiple services, data pipelines, ML models trained and evaluated, real-time features, deployment), working repos or demos, and clear explanation of the design. Published research projects count fully.
**MEDIUM (12-21):** Solid projects with moderate complexity and links, or one strong project plus simpler ones.
**LOW (4-11):** Mostly tutorial-grade projects (todo apps, calculators, basic CRUD, weather apps, standard-library sentiment analysis) or projects without links or detail.
**VERY LOW (0-3):** No projects, or only trivial ones.

### Technical Foundations (0-25 points)
**HIGH (19-25):** Clear command of a primary language and framework shown through work or projects, plus fundamentals across data structures and algorithms, SQL and databases, HTTP and REST, git, testing, and basic containers or cloud deployment.
**MEDIUM (10-18):** Solid in one language and framework with some fundamentals evidenced.
**LOW (3-9):** Skills mostly listed as keywords with little supporting evidence.
**VERY LOW (0-2):** Minimal or no technical skills shown.

### Learning & Initiative (0-15 points)
**HIGH (11-15):** Several strong signals: published research, contributions to other people's open source projects, technical writing or talks, hackathon results, relevant certifications, teaching or community roles.
**MEDIUM (6-10):** One or two clear signals of learning beyond the curriculum or the job.
**LOW (2-5):** Only generic claims of being a fast learner, or a bare GitHub with tutorial repos.
**VERY LOW (0-1):** No evidence of initiative.

## BONUS POINTS (Maximum total: 15 points)
- +3 to +5 for peer-reviewed publications or a substantial research contribution
- +2 to +4 for meaningful contributions to other people's open source projects (personal repos do not count)
- +2 to +3 for senior-style signals shown early: designing a system, owning a service end to end, mentoring peers
- +1 to +2 for relevant certifications (cloud, Kubernetes, security)
- +1 to +2 for hackathon placements or competitive programming achievements
- +1 to +2 for side projects with real users
- +1 for a LinkedIn profile, +1 for a portfolio or GitHub URL in basics

**CRITICAL**: The total bonus points cannot exceed 15 points under any circumstances.

## DEDUCTIONS
- -2 to -4 if the resume is a pure technology keyword list with no described work or projects
- -1 to -3 if all projects are tutorial-grade with no evidence of independent problem solving
- -1 to -2 for projects listed without any link, repo or demo (only if the resume relies on them for its case)
- Do NOT deduct for lack of years of experience, lack of leadership, or lack of large-scale system design
